# EvidenceFirst demo video — 4-minute recording script

## Before recording

- Use an incognito browser at 1440×900 or similar.
- Open the live application, GitHub README, and case study in separate tabs.
- Use a synthetic participant story. Do not expose real participant records or admin credentials.
- Record the screen and microphone. Keep the cursor slow and readable.

## 0:00–0:25 — problem

“EvidenceFirst addresses one narrow problem: people sometimes make costly decisions because of a checkable expectation, while persuasive AI can become inaccurate when it adds more claims. This system first finds the exact reason holding the belief up, then delivers only claims mapped to reviewed sources.”

Show the landing page and click **Start**.

## 0:25–0:55 — consent and discovery

Show the consent disclosure, participant-context choices, and topic/free-text entry.

Say: “The participant is told that this is an AI-assisted study, what is recorded, what is excluded, and that the evidence library may refuse the case.”

Use this synthetic story:

> I did no exercise for seven days while travelling because I believed walking or a short body-weight workout outside a gym could not produce meaningful health or fitness results.

## 0:55–1:35 — adaptive interview

Show two adaptive questions, then move to the confirmation screen.

Say: “The model asks one neutral next question and proposes structured fields. The participant can correct the action and exact expectation before anything is frozen. Deterministic code—not the model—controls qualification and measurement.”

Ensure the confirmed expectation explicitly retains **walking or body-weight activity outside a gym**.

## 1:35–2:10 — eligibility and crux

Show scope, causality, consequence evidence, initial confidence, central reason, and the counterfactual score.

Say: “A case proceeds only when the expectation is current, specific, causal, consequential, checkable, and safe. The counterfactual asks whether the stated reason is actually central. A behavior gap, preference, or unsupported topic is parked.”

Use 9 as the initial score and 3 as the hypothetical score if the reason were false.

## 2:10–2:45 — verified delivery

Show the five-claim brief and open one source in a new tab.

Say: “The final response is not free-written by the model. It is assembled from versioned claim units with exact source URLs and locators. The personalized condition can reorder approved facts around the confirmed reason, while the fixed condition uses the same factual budget.”

Point out the boundary sentence that avoids promising identical gym results or personal consistency.

## 2:45–3:15 — measurement and receipt

Enter a lower post-score, explain what changed, and show the receipt.

Say: “The receipt preserves the exact belief, initial score, confirmed pre-evidence score, after score, delivered content, and a tokenized seven-day follow-up. Immediate change is not treated as durable change.”

Open the follow-up link and show **Not yet due**.

## 3:15–3:40 — refusal and reversal

Show a screenshot or researcher reversal receipt for an unsupported claim.

Say: “A missing match produces no factual claims. The reversal test also checks that supported assertions are preserved, so the system is not simply an argument generator.”

## 3:40–4:00 — architecture and honest status

Show the GitHub README architecture and test status.

Say: “The MVP uses Next.js, Supabase, the OpenAI Responses API, versioned evidence packs, deterministic state control, and immutable receipts. Functional correctness is tested; persuasion efficacy still requires consented comparison data and seven-day outcomes.”

End on the live application URL.
