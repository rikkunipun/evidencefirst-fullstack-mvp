# Pilot script — 15–20 minute sessions

For the owner to run with Sai Teja, Akhilesh, and Anand individually, with consent, on the real deployed app (phone preferred). I (Claude) do not message, recruit, or fabricate these sessions — this script is preparation only.

## Before each session

1. Confirm the participant is 18+ and available for ~20 minutes.
2. Explain: this is a research prototype for a capstone; their answers and scores will be recorded; they can stop at any time; this is not therapy/medical/legal/financial advice.
3. Get verbal or written consent to proceed and to use anonymized results in the submission.
4. Hand them the public URL (or your phone) and step back — do not coach wording.

## What to observe, in order

1. **Can they choose a real decision without help?** Time how long it takes from `/participate` to landing on the first discovery question. Note any confusion on the situation-card or decision-cue screens.
2. **Discovery.** Count questions actually asked. Note any question that felt leading, confusing, or like it assumed an answer.
3. **Read-back.** Did they correct the generated wording? What did they change?
4. **Eligibility questions.** Did any plain-language question feel like a "test" or research jargon leaked through?
5. **If parked:** read the park reason aloud to them afterward and ask if it feels fair/accurate. Record their reaction. This is a valid, useful outcome — do not treat it as a failed session.
6. **If eligible:** baseline score → crux (reason, confirm, hypothetical score, possibly a second reason) → pre-evidence score → "we're reviewing your case" screen.
7. **Researcher review (you, afterward, in `/admin/sessions/[id]`):** decide disposition for real — don't rubber-stamp "supported" just to move the demo forward.
8. **Delivery.** Ask them to read it, then record their own before/after reaction in their own words (don't lead with "did this change your mind?").
9. **Receipt.** Confirm they can see/download it and understand the follow-up link.
10. **Refresh test.** Ask them to close the tab and reopen the link (or refresh) at least once during the session — confirm it resumes exactly where they left off.

## Record for each participant

- Time to choose a decision (unassisted or not)
- Number of discovery questions used (of 8)
- Any correction made to the read-back, verbatim
- Eligibility outcome and, if parked, their reaction to the reason
- Baseline / pre-evidence / post-evidence scores (exact numbers, not paraphrased)
- Their own words for "what changed, failed to change, or complicated your view"
- Anything that felt leading, confusing, or like research jargon
- Device/browser used, and whether refresh/resume worked
- Whether they received the receipt and understood the follow-up link

## Honesty rules for write-up

- Three interviews are usability observations, not efficacy evidence and not a measure of which experimental condition is "better."
- If all three cases park, report that honestly — it is real data about the discovery design, not a failure to find a demo.
- Do not retroactively "clean up" a participant's wording to make the read-back look better than what actually happened.
- Label any case you construct synthetically (for demonstrating the eligible/reversal paths if no real participant reaches them) as synthetic, separately from these three real interviews.
